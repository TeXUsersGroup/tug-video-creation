There were two paths of evolution in writing: one is the modern alphabetic/abugida scripts and the other is the older logo-syllabic script that is still in wide use in China and Japan. The logo-syllabic script traditionally had strong ideographic tendencies that got eroded over time as cultural changes produced a semantic diffusion, as the association between signifier (the form) and the signified (the content) started diverging over time. In this paper we shall study the semiotics of the two writing methodologies from the angle of economy of size in terms of number of glyphs required and the complexity of the glyphs. We illustrate these concepts using the two divergent paths taken by Linear Elamite script and the Indus Valley script.
