In engineering projects, the gap between as-designed and as-built is taken as a given, and often dismissed out of hand. Subsequent iterations of as-renovated or as-bypassed lead to further divergence between state and documentation, raising real risks to the maintenance of critical infrastructure. Audits bring those back into closer alignment.

Transcription surfaces offer convenient data capture methods during the audit process, and can act as primary inputs to initial documentation workflows. Their utility expands further if they are then used as parameterized inputs into controls and automation software generation for managing this infrastructure. As such, their value can subsequently be amortized even more as inputs for creating new end-user documentation.
