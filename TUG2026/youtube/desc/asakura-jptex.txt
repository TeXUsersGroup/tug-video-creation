This talk offers a snapshot of the Japanese TeX ecosystem in 2026, intended for an international audience. It covers what makes Japanese typesetting structurally different from Western typesetting, the two practical workflows in common use today (pTeX variants and LuaTeX-ja), and current challenges around technical debt, kernel-level evolution, and the tentative npTeX concept.

The motivation is to help bridge a persistent information gap. The recent TUGboat overview by Berry, Chernoff, and Hefferon (2025, https://tug.org/TUGboat/ tb46-3/tb144berry-engines-formats.pdf), for example, notes that the (e)(u)pTeX engine “remains very popular in the Japanese community, but is rarely used by others since English-language documentation is limited.” This talk attempts to fill in some of that blank space, offered from the perspective of an observer of both the local Japanese and the global TeX communities.
