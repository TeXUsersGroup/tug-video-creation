Extracting plain text from a LaTeX document is deceptively simple to describe and surprisingly hard to do correctly. This talk will present a family of `strippers' based on a variety of existing tools, from industrial-strength pseudo-parsers like LaTeXML and Pandoc, through home-grown Perl and Python strippers, to a new approach that sidesteps the fundamental problem entirely by letting TeX parse its own output.

The new stripper LuaLaTeX package hooks into LuaTeX's node-list pipeline via pre_linebreak_filter, reading fully-processed paragraph text after all macro expansion, language switching, and font-specific ligature formation have already taken place. The result is exact per-language text extraction, separated by BCP-47 tags, with correct attribution under both Babel and Polyglossia, recovery of discretionary ligatures under both the Lua and HarfBuzz renderers, footnote extraction, and BibLaTeX citation handling, all produced as a zero-overhead side effect of a single compilation.

The companion LaTeXML, LaTeX::TOM, TexSoup and Pandoc-based tools are also presented for cases where TeX compilation is not available, using a three-stage Perl/Python pipeline with balanced-brace-aware pre filtering, a Lua math filter, and a language-marker post-processor.
