{Paulo Ney de Souza}
{A family of strippers\Dash from regular expressions to the real deal}
{Extracting plain text from a \LaTeX\ document is deceptively simple to
describe and surprisingly hard to do correctly. This talk will present a
family of `strippers' based on a variety of existing tools, from
industrial-strength pseudo-parsers like \LaTeXML\ and Pandoc, through
home-grown Perl and Python strippers, to a new approach that sidesteps
the fundamental problem entirely by letting \TeX\ parse its own output.

The new \texttt{stripper} \LuaLaTeX\ package hooks into \LuaTeX's
node-list pipeline via \texttt{pre\char`_linebreak\char`_filter},
reading fully-processed paragraph text after all macro expansion,
language switching, and font-specific ligature formation have already
taken place. The result is exact per-language text extraction, separated
by \acro{BCP}-47 tags, with correct attribution under both Babel and
Polyglossia, recovery of discretionary ligatures under both the Lua and
HarfBuzz renderers, footnote extraction, and \BibLaTeX\ citation
handling, all produced as a zero-overhead side effect of a single
compilation.

The companion \LaTeXML, LaTeX::TOM, TexSoup and Pandoc-based tools are
also presented for cases where \TeX\ compilation is not available, using a
three-stage Perl\slash Python pipeline with balanced-brace-aware
pre\hyph filtering, a Lua math filter, and a language-marker post-processor.}
