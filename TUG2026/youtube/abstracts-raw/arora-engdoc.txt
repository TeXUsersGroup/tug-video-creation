{Transcription surface terraforming: Engineering~documentation~\programrlap{workflows}}
{In engineering projects, the gap between \textit{as-designed} and 
\textit{as-built} is taken as a given, and often dismissed out of hand.  
Subsequent iterations of \textit{as-renovated} or \textit{as-bypassed} 
lead to further divergence between \textit{state} and 
\textit{documentation}, raising real risks to the maintenance of critical 
infrastructure.  Audits bring those back into closer alignment.

\textit{Transcription surfaces} offer convenient data capture methods during
the audit process, and can act as primary inputs to initial documentation
workflows.  Their utility expands further if they are then used as
parameterized inputs into controls and automation software generation for
managing this infrastructure.  As such, their value can subsequently be
amortized even more as inputs for creating new end-user documentation.
}
