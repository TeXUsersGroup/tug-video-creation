{John C. Bowman}
{Asymptote: Past, present, and future}
{Asymptote has undergone substantial evolution in recent years,
spanning language enhancements, rendering backends, and web
deployment. New language features\Dash including templated imports, the
\texttt{using} and \texttt{autounravel} keywords\Dash have simplified coding.
A collections library, contributed by Charles Staats, introduces
hash maps, B-tree maps (which maintain sorted order), hash sets, sorted
sets, and queues as templated modules, along with iterator utilities
such as \texttt{zip} and \texttt{enumerate}. These are supported by
language-level additions: user-defined structs can now be iterated with
range-based for loops via an \texttt{operator iter} method, support
bracket indexing through \texttt{operator[]} and \texttt{operator[=]},
and benefit from native hashing of ints, strings, reals, and int arrays.
Together, these features broaden the range of algorithms that can be
expressed concisely in Asymptote.

The Asy\GL\ library has made it straightforward to embed interactive
\acro{3D} figures directly in \HTML, allowing Asymptote scenes,
including Web\GL\ animations, to be displayed on tablets and phones.

The rendering pipeline has seen major changes: the legacy
Open\GL\ renderer is being superseded by a Vulkan port to support
order-independent transparency across all major platforms, including
\macOS. The upcoming migration from Web\GL\ to Web\GPU\ will bring that
same order-independent transparency to web brows\-ers.

Additional highlights include the Qt6 upgrade of the Xasy graphical
front end, image-based lighting, B\'ezier triangle patches, a portable
compressed \acro{V3D} file format that can be embedded in \PDF{}s, and a
\acro{V3D}-aware plugin for Okular. We survey these developments,
demonstrate live examples, and discuss what lies ahead for Asymptote as
a vector graphics language designed to complement \TeX{} for technical
illustration.
}
